## What this lane builds

1. **The two-lookup composition** ("arm B" of #66): a second LKP instance (unshifted, one function, keys in parts, table {0 .. 2^12 - 1}) whose keys are cells of row a of the product oracle itself (message 0), next to RNG's digit lookup, in one proximity-test group. Exact 12-bit remainder limbs replace exact remainder bits.
2. **Stage 1:** that composition on the M1b statement (one head, D = 128, T = 4, F = 12, public o, no conv, no norm), through a verifier entry that takes statement words.
3. **Stage 2:** the M2 head (T = 16, F = 12): recurrence plus gated RMSNorm proved; public conv history H; conv, SiLU, q/k L2 norm, gates and the norm coefficient cg recomputed by the verifier from pinned integer tables; one verifier entry that checks its own inputs.

Design choices fixed now:
- **Order on the chain.** Message 0: a, b, c. Message 1: fold mask. RNG draws Theta, then sends message 2 (digits) and message 3 (multiplicities). Message 3 holds BOTH instances' multiplicities in two disjoint, fixed place ranges: RNG's planes from place 0, the remainder lookup's planes from place 2^(L-1) (L = table log-length). Then the digit lookup runs (its shift and its alpha), then the remainder lookup (its own alpha), then RNG's batched row, then the proximity test. Every message that holds keys or multiplicities of either instance (0, 2, 3) is therefore sent and sampled before the first challenge of either lookup.
- **What "separated transcript labels" means here.** The library gives every use of a block the same round names ("LKP", "PSC round", ...), and a plan's per-use label is not hashed. This lane does not change the library. Separation is therefore: (i) each instance's public inputs are bound into the first query under its own item name ("range check: digit lookup" for the RNG shape from which RNG derives its lookup, "remainder lookup" for the second LkpPublic), together with the key layout and the first place of the remainder planes; (ii) each instance's challenges are drawn in its own rounds of one hash chain, so the remainder lookup's alpha is a hash of everything before it, including the digit lookup's whole transcript; (iii) the run has its own chain label. H1 tests exactly these three, and the write-up says so.
- **Verifier.** Library `rng_verify` and `lkp_verify`, unchanged. Research copies exist on the prover side only (hostile provers).
- **Layout** as count_model.py arm B: RNG matrices (16 columns, row counts in pieces of at least 1,024) and key parts (pieces of at least 16,384) first, largest first, each aligned to its size; gates after.
- **Norm lowering (stage 2):** DESIGN.md 4.4, with the per-token closed forms of the fourth review: 4 D^2 + 4 D + 2 gates, D^2 + 5 D range cells, D^2 + 7 D + 13 key cells, 9 D^2 + 13 D + 8 relations, plus D^2 terminal relations once.
- **Derived-coefficient admission (stage 2, from the fourth review):** the entry checks on the recomputed integers, before any relation or field conversion: a, beta in [0, 2^F]; |k|, |q| <= 2^F; |v| < 2^31; |cg| < 2^24. A violation is a returned reject at stage "admit". A table input outside its table's domain is also an "admit" reject (no clamp).
- **Pinned tables (stage 2):** the F = 12 files of the operators lane (PR #67): `silu-f12`, `sigmoid-f12`, `decay-one-f12`, 4,096 signed 64-bit little-endian rows each, row = floor(X / 16) + 2048, domain [-8, 8). Copied byte for byte with their SHA-256 recorded and checked by a test. The format is not forked. The q/k L2 norm and the gated norm use the exact integer isqrt of DESIGN.md (no table), because #66 pins that rule and the operators lane's `l2-128` table is a different, bounded approximation. Decay is `decay-one` (A = 1) keyed by ar + dt_bias with a public integer dt_bias. cg_j = floor(G_j * silu[z_j] / 2^12) with G_j a public head constant at 2^12 (sqrt(128) folded in, default 46,341), in checked arithmetic.

## Hypothesis
The two-lookup composition is buildable on the merged M1b machinery and sound, at the research security level, against replayed, relabelled and spliced lookup transcripts; it replaces the exact remainder bits of M1b without losing a rejection; its counts equal the #66 count model for arm B; and the M2 head at T = 16, F = 12 proves and verifies with it.

- **H1 (composition).** The two instances have separate bound inputs and separate challenges, and no tested replay, relabel or splice is accepted.
- **H2 (stage 1).** On the M1b statement with lookup remainders, honest proofs are accepted and every hostile case is rejected.
- **H3 (counts).** The built statements have the #66 arm B counts.
- **H4 (stage 2).** The M2 head at T = 16 proves and verifies through its entry, and a changed in-class conv history H is rejected at the proof.

## Metric

## Acceptance threshold
- **H1 PASS** if all hold: (a) the two alphas differ in 10/10 honest runs; (b) the relabel control rejects; (c), (d), (e) every case rejected. Any accepted case is a FAIL of the composition.
- **H2 PASS** if 10/10 honest accepted, 100% of hostile cases rejected (controls excepted, which must give their registered verdict), and 0 sanitizer reports.
- **H3 PASS** if every compared count is exactly equal (tolerance 0) for each stage that was built. A stage that was not built is reported as not measured.
- **H4 PASS** if 10/10 honest accepted at T = 16, the changed-H case is rejected at the proof stage, every other hostile case gives its registered verdict, and 0 sanitizer reports.
- **Extrapolation check:** the #66 estimate "held" if the measured median prove time of the M2 head is within 25% of 39.2 s (29.4 s to 49.0 s) with table log-length 21. Outside that, it did not hold, and the write-up says by how much.

## Prior art
- This repository: M1 #47, M1b #63 (RNG/LKP composed with the wide product oracle, remainders as exact bits), the M2 design #66 and its four reviews, the operators lane's pinned tables (#67), blocks/iop/lkp.h (lookup by product trees, keys in parts, keys never sent: Papini and Haboeck ePrint 2023/1284; Bootle et al. ePrint 2018/380 appendix B.5; Diamond and Posen ePrint 2023/1784 for virtual key vectors), blocks/iop/rng.h (Lyubashevsky, Nguyen, Seiler ePrint 2020/1448; Gentry, Halevi, Lyubashevsky ePrint 2021/1397).
- Literature: several lookup arguments over one commitment are standard (Halo2 and Plonkish systems batch many lookup arguments under one transcript; Lasso and Jolt decompose into many subtable lookups; zkLLM's tlookup). The known failure classes are weak Fiat-Shamir (Dao, Miller, Wright, Grubbs, ePrint 2023/691: a challenge that does not depend on everything the prover has fixed) and shared randomness between sub-arguments whose inputs are not all fixed. What is new here is narrow: this repository's first composition of two LKP instances in one chain, one of them keyed on product-oracle cells, with a measured cost at the Qwen DeltaNet head shape.

- **Changed H.** H4 registered "H changed in class (must reject at the proof stage)". The check shows that adding 1 to an H word changes no recomputed value: the conv output is floor(acc / 2^12) and the SiLU table key is floor(c / 16), so small input changes are absorbed. The derived statement is then the same statement, it is true for the changed H as well, and the entry is expected to ACCEPT it. That is correct behaviour for this lowering, but it is not what the registered sentence says. The H4 case is therefore run as two cases, chosen by a rule and not by outcome: (i) the least power-of-two increment of one fixed H word (tau = 0, the v conv, channel 0) that changes at least one recomputed value (32 on seed 1): must reject at the proof stage; (ii) the same word plus 1: reported with its verdict, expected accept, labelled as a same-statement case. H4's changed-H criterion is judged on (i), and RESULTS.md will state that "a changed H is rejected" holds only for changes that alter a recomputed value. The same rule picks the xq, ar and z cases.
