## What this lane builds

1. **The two-lookup composition** ("arm B" of #66): a second LKP instance, unshifted, one function, keys in parts, table {0 .. 2^12 - 1}, whose keys are cells of row a of the product oracle itself (message 0), next to RNG's digit lookup, in one proximity-test group. Exact 12-bit remainder limbs replace exact remainder bits.
2. **Stage 1:** that composition on the M1b statement (one head, D = 128, T = 4, F = 12, public o, no conv, no norm).
3. **Stage 2:** the M2 head (T = 16, F = 12): recurrence plus gated RMSNorm proved, public conv history H, conv, SiLU, q/k L2 norm, gates and the norm coefficient cg recomputed by the verifier from pinned integer tables, one verifier entry that checks its own inputs.

Fixed design choices (registered now):
- **Order on the chain.** Message 0: a, b, c. Message 1: fold mask. RNG draws Theta, then sends message 2 (digits) and message 3 (multiplicities). Message 3 holds BOTH instances' multiplicities: RNG's planes from place 0, the remainder lookup's planes from place 2^(L-1) (L = table log-length), two disjoint fixed ranges. Then the digit lookup runs (its shift and alpha), then the remainder lookup (its own alpha), then RNG's batched row, then the proximity test. So every message that holds keys or multiplicities of either instance (0, 2, 3) is sent and sampled before the first challenge of either lookup.
- **Binding.** Each instance's public inputs enter the first query under its own item name: "range check: digit lookup" (the RNG shape, from which RNG derives its LkpPublic) and "remainder lookup" (the second LkpPublic), plus the key cells' layout and the first place of the remainder planes. The run has its own transcript label.
- **Verifier.** Library `rng_verify` and `lkp_verify`, unchanged. Research copies exist on the prover side only (hostile provers), and in one control verifier for the swapped-order case.
- **Layout** as count_model.py arm B: RNG matrices (16 columns, row counts in pieces of at least 1,024) and key parts (pieces of at least 16,384) first, largest first, each aligned to its size; gates after.
- **Norm lowering:** DESIGN.md 4.4, with the scalar and per-channel gates of the fourth review's closed forms (per token 4 D^2 + 4 D + 2 gates, D^2 + 5 D range cells, D^2 + 7 D + 13 key cells, 9 D^2 + 13 D + 8 relations, plus D^2 terminal relations once).
- **Derived-coefficient admission (from the fourth review, registered before code):** the stage 2 entry checks, on the recomputed integers and before any relation or field conversion: a, beta in [0, 2^F]; |k|, |q| <= 2^F; |v| < 2^31; |cg| < 2^24. A violation is a returned reject (stage "admit"). Table inputs outside a table's domain are also "admit" rejects (no clamp, as the operators lane's format says).
- **Pinned tables:** the F = 12 files of the operators lane (PR #67, commit 25213aea): `silu-f12`, `sigmoid-f12`, `decay-one-f12`, 4,096 signed 64-bit little-endian rows each, row = floor(X / 16) + 2048, domain [-8, 8). Copied byte for byte, with their SHA-256 recorded and checked by a test. The format is not forked. The q/k L2 norm and the gated norm use the integer isqrt of DESIGN.md (exact integer arithmetic, no table), not the operators lane's bounded `l2-128` table, because #66 pins the isqrt rule. Decay uses A = 1 with a public integer dt_bias added to the key (the operators tables carry no learned A).
- **cg rule:** cg_j = floor(G_j * silu[z_j] / 2^12), G_j a public head constant at 2^12 with sqrt(128) folded in (default 46,341), computed in 128-bit and checked.
