# RESULTS: 20261104-36-steering-real-proofs

Part of #36. Measured on one rented H100 (synthetic weights only), unreplicated. Every decision below is a
real `cp_prove` outcome and, where a proof was written, a whole `cp_verify` call against the stock
commitment. It is not a Python score. `numbers.json` holds every request. `h100/<s46|g46>/` holds the
raw prover logs, `results.tsv` (name, prover outcome, verifier exit, last line), `result-v1.txt`, the commitment ID
and the proof digests. Proof files were not kept, since they are reproducible and too large.

## Verdict vs PREREG

| clause | threshold | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| strict (S, exact match) | 0 changed steered replies accepted, every unchanged reply accepted | changed: 0 of 57 accepted (57 refused by the prover); unchanged: 13 of 13 accepted (`YES`) | **PASS** |
| lenient (G, shipped clipped-gap) | at least 1 changed steered reply accepted WITHIN LIMITS | 5 of 54 changed replies accepted (`WITHIN LIMITS`), 49 refused | **holds** |
| score agreement | Python score equals prover+verifier decision on every request | 140 of 140 agree, 0 disagreements | **PASS** |
| stock false rejections | dose 0 accepted 5/5 per policy and length | S 10/10, G 10/10 | **PASS** |

Hypothesis direction (smallest dose where every changed reply is refused): at 4 tokens strict and lenient
both refuse all changed replies from |dose| 0.2 up, and lenient accepts one changed reply at -0.05. At 16 tokens
strict refuses every changed reply at every nonzero dose. Lenient accepts 4 changed replies at |dose| 0.05
and refuses all from 0.2 up. So lenient is looser than strict at the smallest tested dose on both lengths.
The hypothesis said "the same at 4 tokens". On the real prover that half is **not supported** (one
4-token case, seed 1105 dose -0.05, 2 changed tokens, is accepted under lenient). CPU #46 had 0
lenient acceptances at 4 tokens, but under another configuration's scales (see below).

**S and G are not paired.** Each policy's steered replies are generated by the integer model of its own
configuration, with its own scales (`KL.bin`). The stock greedy reply differs between S and G in all 10 of 10
(seed, length) pairs (`paired.json`). So "lenient is looser than strict" above compares different replies of
two different integer models, not one reply under two policies. A paired fact from the same data: under G's own
scales, exact match (every cost exactly 256) would accept **0 of G's 54 changed replies**, because each of them has
at least one cost above 256. That is the Python score rule, which agreed with the real decisions on 140 of 140
requests here. It is not a separate prover run. Recomputed by `paired.py` from the committed files, output in
`paired.json`.

No case reached the verifier as a forged acceptance. Every steered reply that fails the limits is refused
by the honest prover (`<name>.failed`, empty). The verifier was never handed a proof of a reply outside
the limits, because an honest prover makes none. So this run measures the policy at the prover, with
cp_verify confirming each written proof. It does not measure a cheating prover.

## What the 5 accepted changed replies look like

All five are at |dose| 0.05. Their per-token costs (the integer model's logit gap + 256, from `requests.json`)
have one or two positions above 256 and every other position at exactly 256. One steered token pushes
the context off the stock path, and from there the stock model's own first choice matches the steered
continuation. So 12 to 14 of 16 tokens differ from the stock reply, yet only 1 or 2 cost anything:

| request | tokens changed | costs above 256 |
|---|---:|---|
| G_s1103_n16_dp005 | 14 of 16 | 1,295,539 and 618,549 |
| G_s1105_n4_dm005 | 2 of 4 | 781,059 |
| G_s1105_n16_dm005 | 13 of 16 | 781,059 and 1,068,754 |
| G_s1106_n16_dm005 | 12 of 16 | 3,580,207 |
| G_s1106_n16_dp005 | 12 of 16 | 786,843 and 958,038 |

This is the steering form of the #38 "one swap then continue" weak point. The limit that lets these through is
the theta mean bound, not cap. Every cost of every G request that got a proof is below C = 9,244,130 (largest
3,580,207), and `above-clip=0` on all 21 G `result-v1` lines, so cap = 1 was never used. Each of the 5 passes on
mean(min(cost, C)) <= theta = 323,343 alone. For example G_s1105_n4_dm005 has mean (781,059 + 3 x 256) / 4 =
195,457. The other four means are 119,854, 115,837, 224,003 and 109,279 (`paired.json`). One or two costs of up
to about theta x len fit the mean, and 16 tokens absorb more than 4. "Number of tokens different from stock" is
therefore not what the policy bounds. For #38 the weak point here is theta x len, not the cap count.

## Differences from CPU #46, stated

- #46 scored the lenient column under examples/accepted-proof's scales (the roomy configuration's
  `KL.bin`), with limits fitted under target_small_f2_gap's scales. Here the lenient commitment is
  target_small_f2_gap itself, and the steered replies are generated and scored under its own scales.
  That is why the lenient 4-token row differs from #46's (0 acceptances there).
- 16-token replies are new here.
- The doses are the brief's minimum grid (0, +/-0.05, +/-0.2, +/-1.0), not #46's full grid.

## Host

The 2x H100 host was an unverified marketplace listing and appeared CPU-throttled (the #43 workload on its other
GPU ran much slower than on the H200 of the same rental, see PR #44). That does not affect this result:
every decision here is deterministic and nothing in this experiment is timed. Do not read the wall times in
`stages.log` as an H100 baseline.

## Not shown

- No cheating prover. A malicious prover would try to make a proof for a reply outside the limits. This
  run only shows the honest prover refusing such replies and the verifier accepting what it proves.
- One neuron (layer 0, shared down column 0), one synthetic 8-layer model, prompt length 8, 5 prompts.
  Not a real model, not a persona or refusal direction, not a detection bound.
- Weight-steered variants (#46's projected and column-doubled artifacts) were not committed. Those IDs need
  the variant weights in a supported weight-file format, which this run did not build.
- Proof files were not kept, on the box or in the pulls, so the 5 accepted changed proofs cannot be committed.
  cp_verify's 34 decisions can be audited through `results.tsv`, `result-v1.txt` and `proofs.sha256`, not re-run
  from the repo. Re-running needs a fresh commitment, which gets a new ID.
- Not replicated. Same-code reruns on another H100 would be a reproduction, not independent replication.

## Reproduce

CPU (any machine, repo root, Python 3.13, torch 2.9.1, numpy 2.3.5):
`python experiments/20261104-36-steering-real-proofs/gen_requests.py OUT` writes `OUT/requests.json` and
`OUT/{S,G}/in-*/`. The PR #50 review regenerated it on Python 3.12, torch 2.14 and numpy 2.5 and got a
byte-identical `requests.json`. On one H100 with the programs built (docs/quickstart.md, "3. GPU"): for each kit,
`cp_commit -weights made-up-blocks -config <kit> -M 1`, then `cp_prove -isk ... -in OUT/<cfg>/in-k -out ...`
per folder, then `cp_verify <config> <ivk> <req> <y> <proof> -id <ID> -policy <policy> -q` per proof.
`run_small.sh` (in this folder) is the rental script that did exactly this. Finally
`python analyze.py OUT/requests.json S/results.tsv G/results.tsv numbers.json`. `python paired.py` (CPU, reads the
committed files of this folder) writes `paired.json`.
